About

Stoop brings you closer to the people nearby.

Need to borrow a ladder? Find a sitter? Meet another family at the park? Text Stoop. It finds the neighbors who can help, even if you haven’t met yet.

Why we’re building Stoop

Most of us would happily lend a tool, recommend a plumber, or say hello at the park. The hard part is knowing who to ask.

We’re building Stoop to make that first connection easier. We think technology should help us spend more time with the people around us.

It goes both ways

Tell Stoop a little about your home, your family, and what you’re happy to share. It might find you a neighbor with a ladder today, then ask you about your trusted electrician tomorrow.

You hear about things that matter to you. And when you can help someone else, Stoop makes it easy.

We’re starting with neighborhoods

The bigger idea is simple: help people share what they know, what they have, and what they can do for each other. We’re starting with the people just a few doors down.

FAQ

What can I ask Stoop?

The everyday things you’d ask a neighbor. Who fixed your leaky roof? Does anyone have a drill you can borrow? Are any families heading to the park? You can also offer something: spare moving boxes, garden tools, or a recommendation you’d happily pass on.

How does Stoop know who to ask?

Neighbors tell it. When you get started, Stoop asks a few questions over text: a little about your home and family, a plumber you trust, or tools you’re happy to lend.

You can share more as things come up—spare moving boxes, a great electrician, or wanting to meet other parents. Stoop remembers what neighbors share, so when someone needs a hand, it knows who might be able to help.

Is Stoop an AI?

Not yet. Right now, it’s me, Marc, behind the number. I read your messages and connect you with neighbors who can help.

I’m building toward an AI-powered community switchboard, but I’m starting by making those connections personally and learning what’s useful.

Does my message go to everyone?

No. Stoop looks for the neighbors who are likely to be able to help and asks them. Your request doesn’t go into a neighborhood group chat.

Will I get lots of messages?

Stoop reaches out when there’s something relevant to you: an answer to your question, something a neighbor has shared, or a request you might be able to help with.

Will neighbors get my name and number?

Stoop asks both people before making an introduction. You don’t have to share your contact details with the whole neighborhood to take part.

Do I need to download an app?

No. Stoop lives in your messages. Send a text to get started.

Once I know my neighbors, do I still need Stoop?

Maybe you won’t. And that would be great. If an introduction turns into a neighbor you text directly, borrow a ladder from, or meet at the park, Stoop has done its job. You can take it from there.

There will still be people nearby you haven’t met. When you need a hand, have something to share, or want to get to know someone new, Stoop can help you find each other.

Is Stoop in my neighborhood?

We’re starting one neighborhood at a time. Leave your number and street address and we’ll text you when your street is on.

How do I get in touch?
